How I work

How do you lead?

As a player-coach. I set the technical direction and the standards, then stay close enough to the work to review the hard implementations, unblock people, and step into a production problem when it helps. I lead through managers as a team grows, developing leaders rather than routing everything through me, but I never drift so far from the code that I lose the judgment to tell good technical decisions from expensive ones.

How do you actually use AI with a team?

Every day, and deliberately. I build production agentic systems myself (multi-agent orchestration, RAG, tool calling, MCP), so I know where AI earns its keep and where a deterministic workflow is the better call. With a team, adoption is a people problem more than a tooling one: I find a champion, pair, train, and measure against cycle time and quality rather than lines of code. And I put the guardrails in first (evaluation, review discipline, least-privilege access, human-in-the-loop) so AI stays a multiplier instead of a cleanup generator.

How do you hire and grow engineers?

I set a clear bar and protect it, then invest hard in the people I bring in. I've grown teams from one engineer to fifteen and directed 50+ through their managers, so I've learned to develop managers and senior leaders, not just individual contributors. I give direct, specific feedback, I create room for people to take on things slightly beyond their current level, and I treat retention as a design problem: reducing knowledge silos so a team stays resilient through growth and change.

How do you modernize a platform without stopping the business?

Incrementally, and with a visible plan. I've led cloud modernizations, legacy consolidations, and acquired-product integrations where the business had to keep running the whole time. I map the technical debt, sequence the work so each step ships value and nothing goes dark, and I'm honest about when to extend, when to refactor, and when to replace. The goal is to make the platform stronger as it scales without ever handing customers a disruption.

You've been a founder. Why take a role instead of starting another company?

Because the work I most enjoy is building the product and the team, and I can do that inside one company I believe in without needing to own the cap table to feel ownership. As a founder I sat at the leadership table and carried outcomes and unit economics directly, so I bring that operator's mindset to a role. I just get to point all of it at one mission instead of running the whole business at once.

How do you work as a remote, distributed leader?

It's how I've worked for years: teams across the US, India, and Europe, led through their leaders. Remote-first rewards clarity: good written communication, lightweight process for planning and incidents, and trust with accountability rather than presence as a proxy for progress. I'm a US citizen based in Barcelona with full EU work authorization, so I can join teams on either side of the Atlantic without sponsorship.

What are you looking for in your next role?

A real leadership seat where I can be hands-on, at a company where AI is genuinely part of how the product and the team get built. I do my best work in regulated or operationally complex domains (fintech, proptech, healthcare-adjacent, data platforms) and at the stage where growth is stretching how the team works and someone has to build the operating rhythm for what's next. I care most about the mission, the people, and getting to do the work, not the title.

Why do you stay hands-on as a leader?

Because it's how I keep honest judgment and credibility. Building things myself is how I can tell the difference between AI that's buying real leverage and AI that's quietly creating a cleanup job later, how I stay current on the thing that's changing fastest, and how I keep the trust of the engineers I lead. A leader who's drifted away from the work loses the ability to make the calls that matter most.
